# The Generation–Symmetry Theorem: A₄, its SU(2) Closure, and the Z₃ Grading

Created: 2026-06-06

Status: **closed theorem (finite group theory), modulo one explicitly stated
hypothesis.** Unlike the bridge notes in this repository, every step below is a
*proof*, not a numerical check. Each lemma is nonetheless accompanied by the exact
machine-verifiable witness computed in
[`compute/epsilon_a4_two_level.py`](../compute/epsilon_a4_two_level.py), so the
argument is simultaneously hand-proved and machine-checked.

The theorem discharges, as a single mathematical fact, two claims the framework had
previously *assumed* separately:

1. the **two-level (qubit) SU(2) symmetry** that
   [`compute/epsilon_free_action.py`](../compute/epsilon_free_action.py) used to
   force the free spurion action (residual **R2**); and
2. the **three-fold generation grading** Z₃ underlying N_gen = 3 (residual **R1**).

Both are shown to be consequences of one finite group, A₄, together with the
double cover SU(2) → SO(3). The single remaining hypothesis — that A₄ acts on the
spurion sector at all — is isolated in §6 and **not** claimed to be proved here.

## 1. Standing hypothesis (stated once, used once)

> **Hypothesis (H).** The triality-broken vacuum of the ℂ⊗ℍ⊗𝕆 framework has, as
> its residual internal flavour symmetry, a faithful action of the alternating
> group $A_4$ by orientation-preserving isometries of a real 3-plane
> $V \cong \mathbb{R}^3$ (equivalently, $A_4 \hookrightarrow SO(3)$ as the
> rotational symmetry group of a regular tetrahedron).

Everything in §§2–5 is a theorem **given (H)**. The justification, limits, and
falsifiers of (H) itself are discussed in §6. We make no other assumption; in
particular we do *not* assume a continuous symmetry, a qubit, or any "two-level
system" — those are *derived*.

Throughout, $\pi : SU(2) \to SO(3)$ denotes the standard two-to-one covering
homomorphism with kernel $\{\pm I\}$, given on the Lie algebra by
$\pi_*\!\left(-\tfrac{i}{2}\sigma_a\right) = J_a$, where $\sigma_a$ are the Pauli
matrices and $J_a$ are the generators of rotations about the axes $a\in\{x,y,z\}$.

## 2. The discrete skeleton: $A_4$, its Klein subgroup, and the Z₃ quotient

**Lemma 1 (structure of $A_4$).**
*Under (H), $A_4$ has order $12$ with conjugacy-class sizes $(1,3,4,4)$. It
contains a unique subgroup*
$$
V_4 \;=\; \{\, \mathbb{1},\ r_x,\ r_y,\ r_z \,\},
\qquad r_a = \text{rotation by } \pi \text{ about axis } a,
$$
*which is normal, isomorphic to the Klein four-group $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$,
and equal to the unique Sylow $2$-subgroup of $A_4$. The quotient is*
$$
A_4 / V_4 \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}_3 .
$$

*Proof.* As the tetrahedral rotation group, $A_4$ is generated by a $3$-cycle of
the body diagonals (order $3$) and a $\pi$-rotation about a face axis (order $2$);
closing these under multiplication yields the $12$ even permutations of the four
vertices. The non-identity elements are: three $\pi$-rotations about the face axes
(order $2$) and eight $\tfrac{2\pi}{3}$-rotations about the four body diagonals
(order $3$). The three order-$2$ elements together with the identity form $V_4$:
each $r_a$ has order $2$, and $r_x r_y = r_z$ (composition of two perpendicular
$\pi$-rotations is the third $\pi$-rotation), so $V_4$ is closed and abelian, hence
$\cong \mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$.

*Normality.* $|V_4| = 4 = 2^2$ divides $12 = 2^2\cdot 3$, and $V_4$ is the **unique**
subgroup of order $4$ (the only elements of even order are the three $r_a$, so any
order-$4$ subgroup must be exactly $\{\mathbb{1},r_x,r_y,r_z\}$). A unique Sylow
$p$-subgroup is automatically normal (it is stable under conjugation, since
conjugation permutes Sylow subgroups and there is only one). Hence $V_4 \trianglelefteq A_4$.

*Quotient.* $|A_4/V_4| = 12/4 = 3$, and every group of prime order $3$ is cyclic,
so $A_4/V_4 \cong \mathbb{Z}_3$. The class sizes follow from the element orders:
$\{\mathbb{1}\}$ (size 1), the three $r_a$ (size 3, one class since they are
conjugate via the $3$-cycle), and the eight order-$3$ rotations splitting into the
two classes of $120^\circ$ and $240^\circ$ rotations (sizes $4,4$). $\qquad\blacksquare$

> **Machine witness (Lemma 1).** `tetrahedral_group()` returns $12$ matrices;
> `conjugacy_class_sizes` $= [1,3,4,4]$; `klein_four_subgroup` has order $4$;
> `quotient_is_z3` returns `quotient_order = 3`, `normal = True`. All `[PASS]`.

The factor $\mathbb{Z}_3 = A_4/V_4$ is the **generation grading**: the three cosets
of $V_4$ are permuted cyclically, and this is the same $\mathbb{Z}_3$ that appears
as the rank-three / three-idempotent structure of N_gen = 3
(see [`three_generations_frame.py`](../compute/three_generations_frame.py)).

## 3. Lifting through the double cover: $V_4 \mapsto Q_8$

**Lemma 2 (the Pauli skeleton).**
*The preimage of $V_4$ under the double cover is the quaternion group of order eight,*
$$
\pi^{-1}(V_4) \;=\; \{\, \pm I,\ \pm i\sigma_x,\ \pm i\sigma_y,\ \pm i\sigma_z \,\}
\;\cong\; Q_8 .
$$
*Moreover $\pi^{-1}(A_4)$ is the binary tetrahedral group $2T$ of order $24$, and
$Q_8 \trianglelefteq 2T$.*

*Proof.* For a rotation by angle $\theta$ about a unit axis $\mathbf{n}$, the two
$SU(2)$ preimages are
$$
\pm\Big(\cos\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,I - i\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\,(\mathbf{n}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol\sigma)\Big).
$$
For $r_a$ ($\theta = \pi$, $\mathbf n = \hat a$) this gives $\mp i\sigma_a$; for the
identity ($\theta = 0$) it gives $\pm I$. Hence
$\pi^{-1}(V_4) = \{\pm I, \pm i\sigma_x, \pm i\sigma_y, \pm i\sigma_z\}$, a set of
eight elements. It is closed: $(i\sigma_a)^2 = -\sigma_a^2 = -I$, and using
$\sigma_a\sigma_b = i\varepsilon_{abc}\sigma_c$ (for $a\neq b$),
$$
(i\sigma_a)(i\sigma_b) = -\sigma_a\sigma_b = -i\varepsilon_{abc}\sigma_c
= -\varepsilon_{abc}\,(i\sigma_c)\in \pi^{-1}(V_4).
$$
Writing $\mathbf i = i\sigma_x,\ \mathbf j = i\sigma_y,\ \mathbf k = i\sigma_z$, we
have $\mathbf i^2 = \mathbf j^2 = \mathbf k^2 = -I$ and
$\mathbf{ij} = -\mathbf k = -\mathbf{ji}$, which are exactly the defining relations
of the quaternion group $Q_8$. Thus $\pi^{-1}(V_4)\cong Q_8$.

Since $\pi$ is a $2{:}1$ homomorphism, $|\pi^{-1}(A_4)| = 2\,|A_4| = 24$; this group
is by definition the binary tetrahedral group $2T = \widetilde{A_4}$. As the preimage
of a normal subgroup under a homomorphism, $\pi^{-1}(V_4)$ is normal in
$\pi^{-1}(A_4)$, i.e. $Q_8 \trianglelefteq 2T$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

> **Machine witness (Lemma 2).** `double_cover(v4)` has order $8$ and
> `is_quaternion_group` matches all $8$ Pauli-group elements (`is_Q8 = True`);
> `double_cover(a4)` has order $24$. All `[PASS]`.

## 4. From discrete to continuous: Burnside forces $U(2)$

This is the crux — the step that turns a *finite* symmetry into the *continuous*
two-level symmetry the action note assumed.

**Lemma 3 (irreducibility).**
*The defining representation of $Q_8$ on $\mathbb{C}^2$ is irreducible.*

*Proof.* A nonzero proper invariant subspace would be a line $\mathbb{C}v$ fixed
(up to scalar) by every element of $Q_8$, i.e. $v$ would be a common eigenvector of
$i\sigma_x$ and $i\sigma_z$. But $\sigma_x$ and $\sigma_z$ have no common
eigenvector (they anticommute: $\sigma_x\sigma_z = -\sigma_z\sigma_x$, and a common
eigenvector $v$ would give $\sigma_x\sigma_z v = \lambda\mu v = -\sigma_z\sigma_x v
= -\lambda\mu v$, forcing $\lambda\mu = 0$, impossible for invertible $\sigma$). Hence
no invariant line exists and the representation is irreducible. $\qquad\blacksquare$

> **Machine witness (Lemma 3).** `commutant_dimension(q8) = 1`. By Schur's lemma
> the complex commutant of a representation has dimension $1$ **iff** the
> representation is irreducible. `[PASS]`.

**Lemma 4 (Burnside closure).**
*The complex linear span of $Q_8 \subset M_2(\mathbb{C})$ is the full matrix
algebra $M_2(\mathbb{C})$. Consequently the smallest associative $*$-subalgebra of
$M_2(\mathbb{C})$ containing $\pi^{-1}(A_4)$ is $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ itself, its group
of unitaries is $U(2)$, and its traceless anti-Hermitian part is the Lie algebra
$\mathfrak{su}(2)$ ($\dim_{\mathbb R} = 3$).*

*Proof.* **Burnside's theorem** states that if a set $S$ of operators on a
finite-dimensional complex vector space $W$ acts irreducibly, then the associative
algebra generated by $S$ is $\mathrm{End}(W)$. By Lemma 3, $Q_8$ acts irreducibly on
$\mathbb{C}^2$, so the algebra it generates is $\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{C}^2) =
M_2(\mathbb{C})$, of complex dimension $4$. Explicitly, $\{I, i\sigma_x, i\sigma_y,
i\sigma_z\}\subset Q_8$ is already a $\mathbb{C}$-basis of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ (the
Pauli matrices together with $I$ span all $2\times2$ matrices). Since
$Q_8\subset\pi^{-1}(A_4)$, the same conclusion holds a fortiori for $\pi^{-1}(A_4)$.

The set of unitary elements of $M_2(\mathbb{C})$ is the Lie group $U(2)$; its Lie
algebra $\mathfrak{u}(2)$ consists of the anti-Hermitian matrices, decomposing as
$$
\mathfrak{u}(2) = \mathfrak{u}(1) \oplus \mathfrak{su}(2),
\qquad
\mathfrak{su}(2) = \mathrm{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\, i\sigma_x,\ i\sigma_y,\ i\sigma_z \,\},
$$
the traceless anti-Hermitian part, of real dimension $3$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

> **Machine witness (Lemma 4).** `burnside_span_dimension(q8) = 4` (full
> $M_2(\mathbb{C})$); `su2_lie_algebra_dimension(q8) = 3`. All `[PASS]`.

## 5. Main theorem

**Theorem (Generation–Symmetry).**
*Assume (H). Then the residual flavour symmetry $A_4$ determines, with no further
input:*

1. *(continuous closure)* *a distinguished two-level system $\mathbb{C}^2$ on which
   the group generated by the double cover $\pi^{-1}(A_4)$, closed under the
   associative algebra it spans, acts as the full unitary group $U(2)$ with Lie
   algebra $\mathfrak{u}(1)\oplus\mathfrak{su}(2)$; the orbit space of the
   normalised states is the Bloch sphere $S^2 = SU(2)/U(1)$, on which $A_4$ acts as
   the tetrahedral rotation group;*
2. *(generation grading)* *a canonical exact sequence*
   $$
   1 \longrightarrow Q_8 \longrightarrow 2T \xrightarrow{\;\pi\;} A_4
   \longrightarrow A_4/V_4 \cong \mathbb{Z}_3 \longrightarrow 1,
   $$
   *whose terminal quotient $\mathbb{Z}_3$ is the three-fold generation grading.*

*Proof.* Part (2) is Lemmas 1–2: $V_4\trianglelefteq A_4$ with $A_4/V_4\cong\mathbb Z_3$
(Lemma 1), and $\pi^{-1}(V_4)=Q_8\trianglelefteq\pi^{-1}(A_4)=2T$ (Lemma 2); the
displayed sequence is exact by construction. Part (1) is Lemmas 3–4: the unique
(up to isomorphism) faithful irreducible module of $Q_8$ is $\mathbb{C}^2$ (Lemma 3),
and Burnside's theorem promotes the finite set $\pi^{-1}(A_4)$ to the full algebra
$M_2(\mathbb{C})$, whose unitaries are $U(2)$ and whose traceless anti-Hermitian
generators are $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ (Lemma 4). The state space $\mathbb{CP}^1 = S^2$
of this $\mathbb{C}^2$ is the Bloch sphere, and $A_4 = \pi(2T)/\{\pm I\}$ acts on it
through $SO(3)$ as tetrahedral rotations. $\qquad\blacksquare$

### What the theorem buys

- **Closes R2's "why a two-level symmetry."** The free-action note
  ([`02_action.md`](02_action.md) §6, residual R2) *assumed* a $U(2)\to SO(3)$
  Bloch-sphere symmetry of the rank-one kernel ⊕ complement. The theorem **derives**
  exactly that $SU(2)/U(2)$ from the discrete flavour group $A_4$: it is the Burnside
  closure of $\pi^{-1}(A_4)$. R2's residual contracts from *"why is there a two-level
  symmetry?"* to *"why $A_4$?"* (hypothesis (H)).
- **Unifies R1 and R2 in one object.** The *same* $A_4$ yields both the qubit
  (part 1) and the three generations $\mathbb{Z}_3 = A_4/V_4$ (part 2). The
  framework's two independent assumptions become two faces of one finite group —
  precisely the "one object, many consequences" economy that mathematical rigour
  demands.

## 6. The single residual, stated honestly

The theorem is **conditional on (H)**: that the triality-broken ℂ⊗ℍ⊗𝕆 vacuum
carries a faithful $A_4$ action by isometries of a 3-plane. The theorem does **not**
prove (H); it *reduces* the framework's two separate continuous/discrete assumptions
to this one discrete-group assumption. The honest status of (H):

- **Supporting it.** $A_4 \cong PSL(2,3)$ is the standard, minimal discrete flavour
  group used to organise three generations in the model-building literature; and a
  tetrahedral $A_4$ sits naturally inside the octonionic automorphism data already
  used elsewhere in the repo (the Fano-line / triality structure). The repository's
  $\mathbb{Z}_3$ generation grading (idempotent frame, N_gen = 3) is *consistent*
  with $A_4/V_4$.
- **Not yet proved.** That $A_4$ — as opposed to any other order-12 group, or a
  larger flavour group restricting to it — is *forced* by the triality breaking of
  the CHO vacuum is **open**. Pinning (H) to the octonionic automorphism group
  $G_2$ / the triality $S_3$ is the next obligation; until then (H) is an input, and
  this note is careful to call it one.

**Falsifier.** If the residual flavour symmetry of the broken vacuum is shown to be
a group whose Sylow 2-subgroup is *not* a normal Klein four-group (so that
$\,\cdot\,/V_4 \cong \mathbb{Z}_3$ fails), the generation grading derived here
collapses, and with it the R1–R2 unification.
